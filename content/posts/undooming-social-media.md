i'm currently on a flight to tokyo, and boredom gets me thinking and wandering.

ever since my youngin high school days, i've had mixed feelings about social media, instagram in particular. it comes with great pros and great cons. i really like seeing how my friends are doing and the impromptu shenanigans they get up to in their daily lives, and instagram is really good for that. but i'm also aware that the big tech overlords are making it worse every time. a few people i know have quit, and i really respect that kind of intentional use. some people also quit during intense phases (like exams).

BUT I JUST REALIZED HOW BEAUTIFUL IT IS TO POST AND SHARE REAL LIFE STUFF.

so in this post, i'm sharing the ideal way of using it that i'm trying to uphold, and how to use it strategically.

when i say social media here, i mean platforms you use to either connect with peeps or gain info. the ones i personally use these days are instagram, twitter, and substack (though i still use twitter and substack very minimally lol).

## pros and cons

ok, before diving into the depths of my yaps, here are the pros and cons.

pros
- sharing life is great
- it works as your personal diary/memories too
- if you share things first, you discover stuff you'd never have known otherwise

cons
- the ads are crazy man
- the doomscrolling is crazy man
- your mind gets so occupied it's hard to do deep work
- you probably only care about the happenings of people who are closer-ish or fascinate you anyway

ofc there's more to think about, but here i'm thinking of ways to fight the cons while maxxing out the pros. let's deep dive!

## fighting the cons

the biggest thing is to have a strong blocker. you don't want to spend hours of screentime every day mindlessly consuming slop. block all the ads and reels (honestly reels are funny, but the doomscroll is too big for me to handle).

how you block depends on how you use it:
- **if you mostly just look:** use a social media wrapper app that hides the bad stuff (ads, reels). the ux is bad and it's almost unusable for posting though.
- **if you like to post:** use an app blocker that makes it harder to open (wait x seconds), only lets you open it x times a day, or gives you a dedicated window just for posting (like 1 hr a day).

this one might be me-specific, but mute a lot of people who have less relevance in your life right now. it might sound harsh, but it's what really works for me.

lastly, go through seasons of not using it at all and reassess whether it gives you value (or not). as i'm finishing uni and getting more unc, i can see it just feels different.

## scaling the pros

### the era of non-chalant

maybe it's more my generation, but social media is now only for the very very cool stuff, without the random daily life sharing. people can do whatever they want, but imo the real value is lost, and as a collective we're shifting toward people not posting as chalantly anymore.

for me, the easiest fix is to just care less about what other people think and live the life you really want! but yeah.

you don't have to share everything with everyone. if you want to, that's great, but if it makes posting chalant stuff easier, go the second account/close friends route. i feel like it takes a certain personality, or that kind of post, to really share the funny stuff.

i've gone through periods of posting kinda actively and periods of barely posting for months to years, and looking back, there's real value in making a digital memory and in the interactions you have with people.

### share your thoughts/experiences!!!

there's real value in sharing real stuff these days. people are scared of getting cancelled over little things (ofc unless it's objectively bad). but if you're willing to sacrifice that comfort, i find people relate to you more, and it often leads to even deeper discussions after.

### be a creator!!

don't just mindlessly consume. by creating, you are allowing your taste to develop, be more decisive, and know what works and what doesn't.

## platform by platform

social media is a place of scale, and that can really work for or against you. keep understanding the scale and what each platform can be used for. just don't post slop thoughh.

### instagram

really great at:
- knowing the happenings of those around you
- sharing simple life stuff, like a broadcast rather than 1-to-1 (though it's def not a replacement!)

but be cautious of:
- ads, doomscrolling
- only showing off the ups and playing the comparison game

### twitter

really good at:
- keeping up with tech and the industry in general
- sharing your raw/unfiltered thoughts (i find it healthier, with more productive discussion)
- being text-based, so less dopamine

but be cautious of:
- bots
- engagement ragebait money farming

### substack

really good at:
- the deepest discussion of em all
- sharing your long-form writing and thinking with the masses

for cons, substack is the least bad of the bunch. it just requires you to be in a pure-learning headspace.

### linkedin

prob one of the worst platforms but one of the most rewarding with intention. it's how i got people working on my sfucourses, exchanging ideas, and getting/giving referrals. the scale works when it's for something concrete.

## personal website

not really a social media, but i think a lot about a tweet from David Perell about how social media feeds value recency over timeless quality.

a personal website is made for more of that timeless stuff, and honestly, as a collective it's def better to read more of the long-form stuff.

the downside, and the reason i keep coming back to insta or twitter: it doesn't get any exposure, and people won't open ur site often.

ofc stuff like rss or a newsletter works, but that's still a slow burn. i just find nothing beats the instant randomness of classic social media.

maybe in the future, if there's tech that lets us have a really good social media from a decentralized site, with no ads and no cheap reels by default, that'd be great. but for now, i have to be a hybrid man.

so for now, the hybrid looks like this:
- posting on my personal site makes me less tempted to post a lot on each platform. i like the notion of having my own thoughts on the internet and using whatever tool suits each one best.
- ofc from a leverage standpoint, this might be the least leverage (but also consider what the leverage is even for in the first place)
- and you can always link your website posts on socmed :D (long thoughts live on the site, the link goes on twitter/insta)

## conclusion

quitting entirely is a valid move, and it's way better than being a doomscroller. but i think there's one level above that: using it with intention, sharing real stuff, and block hardd the baddddd.

using it strategically > off the grid > doomscroller. it's like that bell curve meme. so for now, i'm staying a hybrid man.
