Once upon a time back in 2020, there was a boy from Indonesia who was stressed whether he can get a job in Canada, heck not, barely transfer out of Langara to a decent university. Now, he made it out of the hood!!! :DDD

I wrote this post as the last trifecta post after [[sfu-cs-transfer-guide]] and [[getting-your-first-cs-internship]]

## journey
### 0. choosing the college
Back in highschool, I studied sufficiently. Though as with a lot of my peers back then, I didn't grind enough to choose on a specific university to go to. A lot of universities to go to in HK/ SG/ US/ Germany requires you to grind hard on the national test/ O-Level/ SAT/ Language proficiency test during the HS years.

I ended up choosing Langara college in Canada.

What do I need for Langara? nah they basically accept everybody lol. I mean it's a pretty decent decision to save up money for the first 2 years of undergrad. But I would be lying if I didn't claim that going to uni straight up would put you in an advantageous position.

### I: covid online learning
As there was travel restriction, I spent 6 months learning in zoom from 12am-6am almost everyday. It was pretty hard keeping up with my eyes getting cooked at 4am and basically never going outside home at that time. But life was still relatively easier as I still live at home with my indo fam.

### II. langara college
Social life in Langara is basically non-existent, at least in my program. People there are either trying to transfer out of the college or an international student just trying to part-time work maxxing.

I basically just gpamaxx for transfer here.

This is probably the hardest period during my Canadian life. As I am a cheapskate and I realized how expensive is living abroad. I lived in a homestay with German household, ate sandwich as a main food everyday, lost 10lbs, and budgeted only $50 monthly expense for anything outside.

Oh, and I also did my first job at PNE as a grounds maintenance attendant. It looked cool at first, until I learnt that the job consisted of sweeping floors and replacing garbage. 11/10 would recommend to do especially on rainy day.

### III. sfu early game
Summer 2022!! I finally transferred out of langara, moved out of my homestay, and got a TA job at Langara.

Getting the TA job is probably the most lifechanging moment feeling-wise for me, a different kind of feeling that's somehow better than securing my first co-op/ new grad job. I was able to work on stuff that I enjoyed doing and getting paid money that basically cover all my living expenses here (except the tuition ofc). I felt really great that I can be less reliant on my parents.

Early game SFU consisted of me trying to survive classes + part-time jobs and grinding for my co-ops. I was less stressed with my GPA and more stressed with surviving the overall workload.

At around fall 2022, I was already quite stressed out. After the school year, I would have already secured like 96 out of 120 credits. I only need 2 more terms to finish school and would have finished the degree in like 3.5 years. But getting no coop even back then is a recipe for a cooked life. So I prepped hard for the next life chapter.

More in depth at [[term-review-2022summer]] [[term-review-2022fall]] [[term-review-2023spring]]

### IV: first co-op seeking
Until January 2023 comes, and I was released to seek for my first coop (if I can secure) for the summer term. I applied like 60 jobs in 2 weeks. Got 10 interviews (super grateful!), but have to reject most of them as I already secured my first offer from Visier.

Visier was my second interview of all time and Huawei was the first. I barely passed my first-ever int from Huawei and they scheduled for a second round for a "system design interview". quickly noped out after that as I already got the bag :DD.

A lot more can be said during this arc, but my thoughts at that time was perfectly summarized here - https://brianrahadi.com/posts/getting-your-first-cs-internship.

###  V: 2 years of co-op maxxing
For the next 7 terms from Summer 2023 to Summer 2025, I am always in a co-op term except for Fall 2024.

The decision to continue postponing my school didn't come easily and each co-op for me meant something new to try. Even until I get older, I think this arc as the most life-changing of all time.

Having a co-op is literally crazy. You get paid to learn, have a decent money, and lesser life responsibilities. Relative to the harder school and uni years, this arc was a time of rest and the first time in my life I learnt how to properly take care of my body (i.e. gym).

During my reflections, I kept thinking on how I can prep for better places, grinded harder, lifemax harder. But thinking back on life on a greater scale, I had it already so good (aiya so cringe).

I also joined https://sfussss.org/ and https://sfusurge.com/. Joining clubs for me is a means to contribute and friendmaxx. I found that finding friend groups, heck even just friends, to be super hard in cs classes.
### VI: the final stretch
Come last term [[term-review-2025fall]], things are getting scarier as it's the season of new grad job seeking. It was the chillest term school-wise, which was planned for a good reason.

By sheer of everything, I got a reachout from my old manager at SAP to come back to the team. It was the co-op with the best overall experience for me, so I said yes right away. I thank the universe everything every time for this, my new grad job seeking isn't as scary and complicated as the others.

## some unsolicited advices
### on prioritizations
During school, as with a lot of people, I have to tackle a lot of things. This is a different gameplan with highschool. Try to put care outside school as much as you put in school, if not more if doable.

My early SFU years, I got to juggle:
1. part-time job
2. a club or two
3. friendzah
4. school
5. feeding myself
6. side projects
7. gym
8. cleaning (lol)

Note: Ofc, the season is very different and I don't usually take all the things at the same week. The art of managing it is what makes school life beautiful :D.

looking back, my life was way busier then. The things I am really grateful for are the planted small seeds that really paid off later after uni (friends, fitness, etc). It definitely does not take as much time as school as you have to grind for assignments, exams, etc, so if you put these little stuff first and put school on the remainder of the time, you should be able to live a life well-lived.

Not to mention, for the cs gameplan, I see people are doing competitionmaxxing. This was not something I did a lot back then except for big hackathons, but the payoff of this is also huge if you really prep well hard.

### friendships
1. university and joining the clubs to some extent is probably the easiest time to make friends in your life, leverage the hell out of it
	1. I learnt this the hard way when I was doing co-op in toronto, realizing that making friends is just way harder, esp. living in a very multicultural country.
2. do bfs to all over different circles and dfs the one you really wanna cherish for
	1. from major friends, tech clubs, coworking clubs, faith-based clubs, or even sport-based activities. It is really a blessing to easily find people from all walks of life in uni.
3. Every friend exposes a different part of you that you never realize until you begin forming bonds with them. Marinating this fact, it's just super nice for you to have experienced what it's like socializing in diff social settings.
4. if cs, join silicon valley trip! :D

### career
1. look out for friends and clubs that you really cherish hanging with to grind together.
	1. the pattern keeps repeating, people who get good co-ops/ jobs, a lot of them comes from a strong social group/ clubs/ friendships that support each other.
	2. also necessarily does not mean 'grinding' together literally. moreso for me, it's people who keep you in check that keeps you motivated on the process.
2. I want to admit that getting a good job is even a harder game than when I was starting to get my coop.
	1. This is a fact. But one thing that I want to assure, from my exposure and interactions from countless of peeps in uni. Those who put themselves out there, make sicko project, share it to people, and just experiencemaxxing. They will always get what they want.
	2. Thus, build with friends, build in public, just do whatever to put yourself in the arena and be exposed to others.
	3. Jobhunting, esp for some companies, still really relies on referral afterall. And I feel like with this current AI trend, it will even be a much much bigger thing.
3. snowballing is the way to progress. build up stuff until you get enough credibility to land the next one.
	1. so, if you are in CS, think first about really focusing on making project rather than spam apply. lots of game you should play before playing the job application game. I stacked 90 credits, TA job, projects, hackathons, 3.9 GPA for my first co-op and it netted me 12 interviews from 60 apps. (obviously, times are harder now, but hopefully this datapoint can be helpful)
	2. find and play your unfair advantage. I felt at quite a disadvantage when I started out as I spent my first 2 yrs at Langara - meaning I can't really play the club game and I didn't even bother applying for cs jobs with no uni yet on my resume. However, getting a TA job at Langara arguably is the first snowball that I really leveraged to get my first co-op job.
		1. getting the TA job was a journey on its own. It's def only possible if you have a close connection with the prof. Ace the course, use office hours, then when the stars aligned, apply the job with the professor vouching you.
4. coopmax until you know what you want to do after grad and you are sure you can secure the jobs.
	1. I extended my grad years from 5 to 6 and it's the best thing I have ever done as I got to found a club, try out more stuff, and just more breathing years as I also only will have 3 yrs of work permit.
### clubs
1. not all clubs are created equal and I really mean it, if I were to be in a school like SFU, I would really try to join good clubs.
	1. Why? because in a school where (first of all, sorry)! the median of students are not as crazy as UBC or waterloo students. You can only find peeps who cook in clubs.
	2. I'm really grateful to have gained quite some people that I can proudly say is my friend, and almost none of them are from classes lmao. probably a good majority are from clubs.
2. put a goal or stuff that is like 0 to 1 in your uni. There is really not much stuff happening at SFU but lotsa resources to take.
	1. make random event, join competition and mogs it hard, do whatever it takes, and most importantly, use that SFSS money to make stuff that are actually good.

### projects
1. this is quite cs specific, but in a time of AI high, I think it is really the best time of making side projects. MAKE THEM, PUBLISH THEM, GET PEOPLE TO ROAST YOUR STUFF, AND BE HAPPY THAT IN THE END YOU TRIED MAKING SOMETHING
2. compared to other people, I don't really have a grandiose idea of making a really cool thing, I just like to make something that's relative simple but working.
	1. but I think the other way is fine too, whatever floats your boat sir.
	2. I guess focus on what is the end goal that you want. Users? Learning? Resume larping for job? the best project I think can hit all these, so brainstorm really really hard.
	3. It's kinda like hackathon where after joining quite some, you know whether someone is winning or not based on the idea (and execution, but rn with AI, i think the idea and taste wins).
### grades
1. I ended up with a pretty good ending grade, but in the end, grade should never be the end-all be-all (although it is easy to say right now and it still took a huge chunk of time).
	1. Think of it as kind of filling your vase with a big rock, small rock, marbles, sands. Try to put this grade somewhere on the end-ish. (OFC this is like a very very personalized and something that's for me).
	2. So really, what's more important? project, coops, clubs, friends, *networking (rip)*, or even just sidequests. There's more to gain with so many empty timeslots you have in life right now.
	3. Ofc, understanding the material is key!! and school is the time for learning. but I'd argue that learning cs stuff nowadays is way way better if you learn directly from the trendmaker (a.k.a the industry). So invest more time in learning this too! school is so slow and cooked bro.
2. use [sfucourses.com](https://sfucourses.com/) to plan your courses (hehehehehehehehehehehehe)
